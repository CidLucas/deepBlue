# AI in Project Management and Dashboard Creation: A Comprehensive Study Guide

This study guide explores the practical application of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in industrial management, project management, and data visualization. Based on expert insights, it outlines strategies for effective prompting, the integration of AI within established software ecosystems, and methodologies to increase productivity while avoiding common pitfalls in dashboard development.

## Part I: Short-Answer Quiz

1. **What is the primary risk of using superficial prompts when interacting with an AI for data analysis?**
   Superficial prompts increase the likelihood that the AI will fail to understand the specific goal, leading it to provide generic descriptions rather than the requested analysis. This often results in the AI "going everywhere" except the intended destination.
2. **What three fundamental questions must be answered regarding a data source before beginning an analysis or dashboard project?**
   One must determine the nature of the source (database vs. spreadsheet), the period of data feeding/updates, and the amount of time that will be dedicated to analyzing those specific data points.
3. **How can AI be used to "anticipate" solutions even when a real-world problem or database is not yet available?**
   AI can be used to simulate a database or scenario by providing a few key points of a business case. This allows a manager to project solutions and build dashboard structures before having access to real, sensitive company data.
4. **According to the practical test, how did the free version of ChatGPT perform when tasked with generating a robust dashboard file?**
   The free version was described as "evasive" and failed to deliver a conclusive, downloadable dashboard or a robust web view. It eventually reached its usage limit, leading the tester to give it a "5 out of 10" rating for that specific task.
5. **What alternative method was used to improve ChatGPT's understanding when the initial file upload was unsatisfactory?**
   When the file upload didn't yield a satisfactory result, the user copied the raw data from the spreadsheet and pasted it directly into the prompt. This allowed the AI to identify specific formulas and provide a more detailed explanation of the spreadsheet structure.
6. **Why is it recommended to use Microsoft Copilot when working specifically within the Power BI ecosystem?**
   Copilot is natively integrated into the Microsoft 365 and Power BI ecosystem, which ensures that commands are more accurately perceived. While other AIs can be used, native integration allows the project workflow to flow better.
7. **What does it mean for a professional to use advanced tools "as a beginner"?**
   It refers to automating advanced processes so the user does not have to perform repetitive manual labor, such as opening databases daily. The goal is for the tool to work for the person, rather than the person being a slave to the tool's manual requirements.
8. **How does the "Pomodoro" technique apply to the development of Business Intelligence (BI) projects?**
   The technique uses short, timed cycles to focus on specific tasks, helping developers realize if they are spending too much time on a single component. It serves as a "beat" or "timer" to ensure the project continues to move toward a deliverable.
9. **What is the suggested time allocation for the storytelling and graphic design phases of a BI project?**
   Developers should ideally spend no more than 30% of their time on icons, backgrounds, and storytelling. In practice, many spend up to 80% on these visual aspects, which is considered a dangerous diversion from core development and management.
10. **In the context of Scrum, how does the "Minimum Viable Product" (MVP) approach benefit project delivery?**
    The MVP approach reduces global expectations into small, manageable, and deliverable increments. This allows for "small conquests" at the end of each cycle, which are eventually combined into the final product or service.

## Part IV: Glossary of Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Copilot** | Microsoft's integrated AI assistant designed to work within the Office 365, Power BI, and Azure ecosystems. |
| **DAX (Data Analysis Expressions)** | A library of functions and operators used to build formulas and expressions in Power BI and Excel. |
| **Dashboard** | A visual representation of key performance indicators (KPIs) and data points used to monitor and analyze business processes. |
| **Hands-on Methodology** | A practical, "learning-by-doing" approach to training and project execution favored by the source author. |
| **Lovable** | An AI-based tool mentioned as being more "objective" and "competent" in delivering dashboard results compared to the free version of ChatGPT. |
| **MVP (Minimum Viable Product)** | The smallest version of a product that can be delivered to provide value and test a concept within a development cycle. |
| **Pomodoro Technique** | A time management method that uses a timer to break work into intervals, traditionally 25 minutes in length, separated by short breaks. |
| **Prompt** | The specific text input or instruction given to an AI to generate a desired response or output. |
| **Scrum** | An agile framework for project management that emphasizes incremental delivery and iterative progress. |
| **Storytelling** | In the context of BI, the practice of using data visualizations and narrative to communicate insights clearly to an audience. |
